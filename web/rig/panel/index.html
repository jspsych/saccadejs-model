Screen → webcam timing

A gaze sample carries the camera frame's captureTime, but the photons left the screen some milliseconds before that stamp and the camera added more of its own. This page measures that total — the constant to subtract from a gaze timestamp to line it up with what was on screen. The default test only switches the screen between two levels about once a second: no flicker.

Two physical lags stay invisible to JavaScript: display lag (the requestAnimationFrame flip → photons actually leaving the panel) and camera lag (photons → the captureTime stamp), both on the same performance.now() timeline jsPsych uses for stimulus onsets. Their sum is what test 1 measures with no extra hardware: it switches the whole screen between two levels at known times, watches the mean luminance of the webcam image, and cross-correlates the two. The screen lights your face and the room, so this works with a normal user-facing webcam; pointing the camera at the screen gives a stronger signal.

Sparse steps, no flicker. The screen switches between two levels at random moments 0.5–1 s apart — at most one flash per second, a third of the WCAG 2.3.1 / Harding limit of three per second. The timing information is in the edges, not the rate, so ~20 edges over 15 s pin the lag down to a few ms.

⚠ Photosensitivity warning (m-sequence mode). After Start and a 3-second countdown the entire screen will flash between the two levels at up to about 8 Hz for the chosen duration. Do not use this mode if you have photosensitive epilepsy or are sensitive to flashing light.

Test 1 needs no rig. Tests 2 and 3 need the photodiode taped to the screen and the LED in the webcam's view (web/rig); connect it here, aim the LED, then run them.

Aim the LED: the preview is mirrored, so move it the way it looks. The view subtracts a slowly updated background, so anything blinking stands out. Locate LED blinks it three times and marks where it found it — the analysis uses that same on-minus-off contrast, on whichever of the two planes separates better.
